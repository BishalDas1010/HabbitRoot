Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 25, 2026

Last updated: October 25, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how HabitRoot handles information when you use our Android application. Please read it carefully before using the app.

1. Introduction

HabitRoot is a habit-building, productivity, and focus timer application developed by Bishal Das, an independent developer. The app helps users create habits, track progress, manage focus sessions, and build consistent routines.

This policy describes the information the app may process, how that information is stored, the purpose of Android permissions, and the choices available to you.

2. Information We Process

Depending on the features you use, HabitRoot may process the following information:

  • Habit names, schedules, completion status, and progress.
  • Focus session durations, timestamps, and focus statistics.
  • Daily goals, streaks, virtual tree growth, and rewards.
  • App-blocking preferences configured for Strict Focus Mode.
  • Selected focus sounds, app settings, and reminder preferences.
  • Information associated with permissions you grant, where necessary for the relevant feature.

The app is intended to store its habit and focus information locally on your device. However, the actual handling of data depends on the app's implementation and installed libraries. The developer must verify whether any information is transmitted through network requests, SDKs, backups, or other services before making definitive claims about data collection.

Authentication: If authentication is provided, its implementation and any associated information must be verified against the current app version.

3. Information Stored Locally on Your Device

HabitRoot is designed to keep habit records, focus history, progress, settings, and app-blocking preferences on your device. Local storage may use Android databases, DataStore, preferences, or other app-specific storage mechanisms.

Local storage does not automatically mean that information can never leave your device. Android backup settings, exported files, network functionality, and third-party SDKs may affect how information is handled. These mechanisms must be checked in the actual implementation.

4. Accessibility Service and Strict Focus Mode

HabitRoot's Strict Focus Mode may use Android's Accessibility Service to detect when you open an application you have selected for blocking. This helps HabitRoot respond when you attempt to access a restricted application during an active focus session.

Depending on the implementation, the service may process application names, package identifiers, or application-window events on your device to identify whether an app should be blocked. The exact information processed depends on the service's source code and configuration.

Whether any Accessibility Service information is transmitted outside your device has not been established by this policy. This must be verified by reviewing the service implementation, network requests, and third-party SDKs before publication.

Accessibility access is intended to support the app-blocking feature. The developer must verify that the service does not collect or transmit information for advertising, analytics, or unrelated purposes before making that statement.

You can disable Accessibility access through your Android device's Settings, under Accessibility or the installed accessibility services section. The exact menu names vary by device. Disabling access prevents features that depend on the service from operating as intended, including Strict Focus Mode app blocking.

HabitRoot must clearly explain the purpose of Accessibility access before requesting it. This Privacy Policy does not replace any prominent disclosure or consent required by Google Play or Android.

5. Android Permissions

Depending on the permissions declared and requested by the installed version, HabitRoot may use Android permissions for these purposes:

  • Accessibility Service: Supports app detection and blocking in Strict Focus Mode, if enabled.
  • Notifications: May display habit reminders or focus-session notifications, if implemented and permitted.
  • Usage Access: May help identify application usage if the feature is implemented and this access is requested.
  • Internet access: May support network-dependent features or libraries, if present in the app.

These are explanations of possible uses, not confirmation that every permission is present in HabitRoot. The final policy must match the actual AndroidManifest.xml, runtime permission requests, and source code.

6. Data Sharing and Disclosure

HabitRoot is intended to process ordinary habit and focus records locally rather than send them to the developer. However, this must be confirmed by auditing network requests, SDKs, backup behavior, and any backend services.

Do not assume that no information is shared until these checks are complete. If the app shares information with service providers or other parties, this policy must identify the relevant categories of information, recipients, and purposes.

Any legally required disclosure must be handled in accordance with applicable law.

7. Third-Party Libraries and SDKs

HabitRoot may include third-party libraries to support Android functionality. Their presence and behavior must be verified from the project's Gradle dependencies and the final app build.

If libraries for analytics, crash reporting, advertising, authentication, or cloud storage are included, their data practices must be reviewed and accurately disclosed here and in the Google Play Data Safety form where applicable.

8. Internet and Network Usage

Whether HabitRoot makes network requests depends on its permissions, code, and dependencies. The presence of the Android INTERNET permission alone does not prove that personal information is transmitted, and its absence from a manifest alone is not a substitute for reviewing the complete app configuration.

Before publication, the developer must verify whether the app communicates with any server, API, or external service and document the information transmitted and the purpose of each connection.

9. Data Retention and Deletion

Locally stored information generally remains on your device until it is deleted by you, removed by the app, or cleared through Android settings. The exact behavior depends on the storage implementation.

You may be able to delete individual records or reset app data through available in-app controls. Uninstalling the app or clearing its storage generally removes local app data, but exported files and device backups may remain.

The developer must verify all deletion controls and any server-side retention or account-deletion requirements if accounts or remote services exist.

10. Data Security

HabitRoot relies on the app's storage implementation and Android's security features to protect information on the device. No method of storage or electronic transmission can be guaranteed to be completely secure.

Users should protect their devices with appropriate screen locks and keep Android and HabitRoot updated. Any claims about encryption or additional security safeguards must be confirmed against the actual implementation.

11. Children's Privacy

HabitRoot is not intended to collect personal information from children through an undisclosed process. The developer must determine the app's intended age group and applicable legal requirements before publication.

If the app is directed to children or is made available to audiences covered by additional child-safety requirements, appropriate disclosures and safeguards must be implemented.

12. Your Choices and Permission Controls

You can manage your use of HabitRoot by:

  • Editing or deleting habit and focus records using available app controls.
  • Disabling notifications through Android settings.
  • Revoking Accessibility access to stop the service-dependent blocking feature.
  • Revoking other optional permissions through Android settings.
  • Clearing HabitRoot's storage or uninstalling the app.

Available controls may vary by app version. Disabling a permission may prevent the associated feature from working correctly.

13. Changes to This Privacy Policy

This policy may be updated when HabitRoot's features, permissions, dependencies, or data practices change. The effective date and last-updated date at the beginning of this page indicate when the current version took effect.

Please review this page periodically. If a change requires additional notice or consent, the developer will provide it as required by applicable law and platform policies.
